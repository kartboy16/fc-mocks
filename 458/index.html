Directions

Recommended

Direction A · Plain text prefix

The prefix from Settings is typed into the subject for you, followed by one space. It is ordinary text: edit it, delete it, or leave it. Simplest to build and to explain.

Alternative

Direction B · Locked prefix chip

The prefix sits as a chip in front of the subject box. It can't be half-edited by accident; ✕ removes it for this campaign and “Add back” restores it.

Alternative

Direction C · Name + style picker

Settings asks for a name (BPartners) and a style: “From BPartners:”, “BPartners |” or “[BPartners]”, with a live preview. In the campaign it behaves like A.

Why A

It does exactly what BPartners asked with the least new UI: the subject box looks like it does today, just pre-filled. Advisors already understand editing text, and the subject they see is the subject that gets sent. B protects the prefix from half-edits but adds a new control and a second “remove/add back” state to explain. C is worth it only if many firms want different separators; with one client asking today, a free-text field covers “From X:”, “X |” and “[X]” already.

Open questions for Alex

  1. Which new campaigns get it? Recommended: every new campaign made for the account, including ones FTT prepares from Suggested posts or Content Library distribute. Duplicates keep the original subject as-is.
  2. Changing the prefix later: recommended to leave existing drafts and scheduled campaigns alone and only affect new ones.
  3. Who sets it: the advisor in their own Settings (recommended), or FTT staff per firm?
  4. Separator: A/B store exactly what's typed and add one space. Should we also add a colon automatically if they leave it off? (C answers this with a style picker.)
  5. Preheader and test sends: recommended no prefix on the preheader; test sends keep today's “[TEST] ” in front of the full subject.
  6. Mailchimp accounts: apply the same setting there too, or SES (FTT Mail) only for now?
